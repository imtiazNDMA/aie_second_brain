# Instagram Audience and Positioning

## Primary audience

Aspiring AI engineers: software developers, data practitioners, students, and technical builders moving from AI experimentation toward shipping reliable AI systems.

They are usually beginner to intermediate in AI engineering. They can follow code and architecture diagrams but need unfamiliar mathematics, infrastructure, and research terminology explained.

## Their situation

- They know isolated terms but cannot see how the full system fits together.
- Tutorials help them build demos but rarely explain evaluation, reliability, cost, or failure modes.
- Fast-moving model news makes them unsure what is foundational and what is temporary.
- They want portfolio projects and production judgment, not another list of tools.
- They often have limited compute and need realistic paths to practice.

## Desired transformation

```text
"I recognize AI terms"
        ->
"I can explain the architecture"
        ->
"I can build and evaluate it"
        ->
"I can make production tradeoffs"
```

## Positioning

### Category

Practical AI engineering education.

### Point of view

AI engineering is not prompt tricks or model news. It is the discipline of turning probabilistic models into evaluated, reliable, secure, and maintainable systems.

### Differentiation

- Systems over isolated tools
- Mechanisms over buzzwords
- Tradeoffs over universal recipes
- Evaluation over impressive demos
- Visual explanations over dense jargon
- Build evidence over borrowed authority

### Follow promise

Follow to learn one production-grade AI engineering idea at a time, from transformers and RAG to agents, evaluation, inference, and operations.

## Voice

- Clear, direct, and technically serious
- Curious rather than performatively certain
- Accessible without talking down to the reader
- Opinionated only when the reasoning is visible
- No hype, fake urgency, or unexplained acronym walls

Use "you" when describing a learner decision. Use "we" only for genuinely shared reasoning, never to imply a team or experience that does not exist.

## Content promise test

Before drafting, complete this sentence:

> After reading this carousel, an aspiring AI engineer will be able to ___ without ___ .

Examples:

- choose a RAG evaluation metric without confusing retrieval quality with answer quality
- explain self-attention without memorizing an opaque formula
- recognize an unsafe agent architecture without deploying it first

## Authority ladder

Build authority in this order:

1. Explain a concept clearly.
2. Connect it to adjacent system components.
3. Demonstrate it with code or an architecture.
4. Evaluate the result and expose failure modes.
5. Publish the tradeoff and what changed in your understanding.

The goal is not to claim "top creator." The goal is to repeatedly produce the evidence that makes the audience reach that conclusion.

## Scope boundaries

Primary:

- LLM and multimodal architecture
- RAG and knowledge systems
- AI agents and tool use
- Evaluation and observability
- Inference, deployment, and LLMOps
- Reliability, security, and system design
- Build roadmaps for aspiring engineers

Secondary:

- ML and deep-learning foundations needed to understand the primary topics
- Career and portfolio guidance grounded in actual engineering artifacts

Avoid:

- generic AI news summaries
- unsupported income or career promises
- tool roundups without selection criteria
- recycled motivational content
- pretending benchmarks prove production readiness
