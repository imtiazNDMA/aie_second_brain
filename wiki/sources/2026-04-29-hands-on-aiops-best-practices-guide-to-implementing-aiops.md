# Hands-on AIOps: Best Practices Guide to Implementing AIOps

**Source:** annas-arch-37ca78a111a1.pdf  
**Authors:** Navin Sabharwal, Gaurav Bhardwaj  
**Publisher:** Apress (2022)

## Key takeaways

- AIOps delivers the most value when event processing is treated as a lifecycle: ingestion, normalization, suppression, deduplication, correlation, and automated response.
- SRE and DevOps outcomes improve when AIOps is tied to SLOs, toil reduction, and operational runbooks instead of only adding monitoring tools.
- Automated baselining, anomaly detection, and predictive analytics are practical capabilities, but they depend on data quality and domain context.
- Organizational issues (rigid processes, fragmented ownership, weak CMDB/topology data) are major blockers and must be handled as part of implementation.
- Production adoption is best done iteratively with measurable wins, operator feedback loops, and gradual increases in remediation autonomy.

## Notable implementation areas

- Event deduplication and noise reduction workflows.
- Automated baselining using time-series models and drift-aware thresholds.
- Predictive operations for capacity and incident prevention.
- Cross-domain use in AppOps, SecOps, and infrastructure operations.
