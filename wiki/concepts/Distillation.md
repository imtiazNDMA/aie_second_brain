# Distillation

## Definition

**Knowledge distillation** is the practice of training a smaller "student" model to reproduce the behavior of a larger "teacher" model. Originally formulated for classification (Hinton et al. 2015), distillation has become a foundational tool of modern LLM development — used to compress large frontier models into small deployable ones, and (in 2024–2025) to transfer **reasoning capability** from large reasoning models like [[DeepSeek-R1]] into small dense students.

## Mathematical formulation

The classical KD objective combines two terms — a hard-target cross-entropy on ground-truth labels and a soft-target loss matching the teacher's output distribution:

$$\mathcal{L}_{KD} = \alpha \cdot \mathcal{L}_{CE}(S, y) + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \tau^2 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{KL}\big(P_S^\tau \,\Vert\, P_T^\tau\big)$$

The temperature-softened distributions:

$$P^\tau(i) = \frac{\exp(z_i / \tau)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / \tau)}$$

Temperature $\tau > 1$ flattens the distribution, exposing more "dark knowledge" (relative weights between non-top classes) for the student to learn from.

## Three eras of distillation

### Classical KD (2015–2019)
Compress small classifier models for deployment. Teacher and student are typically in the same architecture family.

### LLM compression (2019–2023)
DistilBERT, TinyBERT, MiniLM. Compress encoder/decoder models 2–4× with modest quality loss, primarily for latency-sensitive inference.

### Reasoning distillation (2024–2025)
The 2025 wave (DeepSeek-R1, s1) demonstrates that **reasoning capability transfers** via distillation:

- **DeepSeek-R1's distill family** — six dense models (Qwen-1.5B/7B/14B/32B, Llama-8B/70B) trained on R1's reasoning traces. Distill-Qwen-32B beats o1-mini on multiple math/code benchmarks.
- **s1 (Muennighoff et al.)** — distills Gemini Flash Thinking traces into Qwen2.5-32B with just 1,000 examples. Achieves o1-preview-level math performance for under \$50 in compute.

The recipe shifted from "match teacher logits" to "fine-tune on teacher's reasoning chains" — supervised fine-tuning on (question, reasoning, answer) triples generated by the larger model. Cheaper, more sample-efficient, and broadly applicable.

## When to use which approach

- **Soft-label KL (classical)** — both teacher and student are accessible, same architecture family, classification-style outputs
- **Sequence distillation** — teacher generates target text; student is fine-tuned to match. Standard for reasoning/code transfer.
- **Self-distillation** — student is the same architecture as teacher; iteratively improve via self-generated targets

## Limitations

- **Capability ceiling** — students cannot exceed their teacher (without RL or additional supervision)
- **Distribution dependence** — student inherits teacher's biases and failure modes
- **Long-tail loss** — rare-but-important behaviors disappear at small scale even with good distillation
- **Test-time compute mismatch** — distilling a reasoning teacher into a non-reasoning student is brittle; the student needs to be trained to think long, not just to mimic short answers
